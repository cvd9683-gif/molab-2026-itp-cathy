## What is your favorite mobile app and why?

My favorite app is **Apple Notes**. It's not the flashiest app on my phone, but it's the one I open most often and rely on the most, and I think that says a lot about how well it's designed.

What I appreciate most is how well structured it is without feeling rigid. Opening a new note is fast and there's barely anything in the way, so it's easy to get a thought down before I lose it. At the same time, there's enough organization underneath (folders, checklists, headings) that my notes don't turn into a mess, even though I add to them constantly. It feels simple on the surface but has a lot of depth when I need it.

I also love that it syncs across my laptop and my phone. I can start a note on my phone while I'm out, then open it on my laptop later and keep working. Because of that it doesn't feel tied to one device. It's always there, wherever I am.

The range of things I use it for is probably the best proof that it works. I use it for to-do lists, random ideas, things I notice during the day, and a lot of half-formed thoughts I want to come back to. One tool handles all of those without making me decide ahead of time what kind of thing I'm writing. As a designer, I find that kind of flexible, low-friction experience really inspiring, and it's something I'd like to bring into the apps I make.

## Final project concept

I don't have a set concept yet, and I'd like to keep exploring before I commit. There are two directions I keep coming back to.

**A social app.** I'm interested in how apps shape the way people connect, share, and show up for each other. A lot of social platforms today are built around feeds and constant engagement, and I'm curious what a social experience could look like if it were designed around a more specific, intentional way of being together. I don't know yet what that would center on, but I'd like to find a small, focused interaction that feels meaningful rather than endless.

**Making niche health information more accessible.** I've become really interested in the smaller, more overlooked corners of health, where good information exists but is hard to find, hard to understand, or scattered across sources that aren't built for everyday people. I'd like to explore how a mobile app could make that information clearer and more approachable, so people feel more informed and in control of their own health instead of overwhelmed by it.

These two ideas might come together, for example as a health-focused experience with a social or community element. They might also stay separate. Either way, I'm open to letting the class, the tools, and what I learn about building for iOS shape where the project ends up.
